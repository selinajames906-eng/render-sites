How to read it: the seller counts "8 ingredients" by listing each B vitamin separately, and gives no amount for any of them. The bottle says "Advanced Nerve Support Formula" and "Net Wt. 1 fl oz (30 mL)". There is no serving size or dropper amount on the pages we saw, so the label on your own bottle is the final word.

Seller claim: "nano" technology for better absorption. No evidence is shown for that.

Label amount: amount not given

1. Whole Coffee Fruit Extract

An extract of the whole coffee cherry, studied for a rise in BDNF.

Studied for: One small trial of a branded extract reported a short-term BDNF rise; BDNF is a blood marker, not a nerve outcome.

Worth knowing: Some extracts carry caffeine.

Label amount: amount not given

2. PEA (Palmitoylethanolamide)

A fatty-acid compound the body makes.

Studied for: Small trials for nerve and chronic pain at 300 to 1,200 mg a day, with modest results.

Worth knowing: A liquid dropper dose is not stated.

Label amount: amounts not given

3. Vitamins B12, B6 And B1

Three B vitamins used by nerves.

Studied for: B12 deficiency is a proven cause of neuropathy; replacing it helps.

Worth knowing: Too much B6 can itself damage nerves.

Label amount: amount not given

4. R-Alpha Lipoic Acid

An antioxidant made by the body.

Studied for: Trials in diabetic neuropathy use about 600 mg a day, with modest benefit.

Worth knowing: Can lower blood sugar.

Label amount: amount not given

5. Lion's Mane Mushroom

A mushroom studied for nerve growth factors.

Studied for: Mostly animal and cell studies; small human trials on cognition.

Worth knowing: Mushroom allergy is possible.

Label amount: amount not given

6. Organic Curcumin Longa

A turmeric extract.

Studied for: Small trials for pain, mostly in joints, with absorption aids.

Worth knowing: May affect bleeding and gallbladder problems.
